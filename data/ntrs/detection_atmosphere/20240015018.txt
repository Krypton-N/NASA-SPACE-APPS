<p>Compartment Fire Modeling of a Crew Cabin in 1-g and Lunar-g
</p>
<p>Introduction
• A fire on a spacecraft poses a catastrophic risk
• Large-scale fire growth tests were performed to inform 
</p>
<p>the decision regarding cabin atmosphere for the 
Artemis 3 mission
</p>
<p>• Data from these tests was used to develop a model to 
determine the habitability inside a crew cabin
</p>
<p>• The profile from the top thermocouple at the door was 
used as the heat input of the model and the magnitude 
of the heat input was calibrated until the model 
matched the experiment
</p>
<p>• Other thermocouples at the door were used to validate 
the model
</p>
<p>• Model was simulated in lunar-g to determine the effect 
of gravity
</p>
<p>Objectives
•Use data from tests to estimate 
heat input into crew cabin 
</p>
<p>•Validate numerical model in 1-g 
using test data
</p>
<p>•Use model in lunar-g to estimate 
the effect of gravity on the 
habitability inside the crew cabin
</p>
<p>Justin Niehaus and David Urban
NASA Glenn Research Center
</p>
<p>Experimental Conditions
Nine tests were performed on partial crew 
quarters mockups at White Sands Test Facility to
observe fire growth in exploration atmospheres
</p>
<p>• O2 from 24% to 37%
• Pressure from 8.2 to 12.44 psia
</p>
<p>Ignition from chemical igniter or Surface Pro 
(lithium-ion battery thermal runaway via patch 
heater)
</p>
<p>•A test campaign was completed at WSTF in order to test fire 
growth and fire propagation properties at exploration 
atmospheres
</p>
<p>•Data from the test campaign was used to validate a model in 1-g 
that generated a heat input to match the atmospheric 
temperature
</p>
<p>•The model was simulated in Lunar-g to determine the effect of 
gravity on habitability
</p>
<p>•Air temperature in the lunar-g simulation was substantially 
higher than in 1-g showing buoyant transport significantly 
affects habitability
</p>
