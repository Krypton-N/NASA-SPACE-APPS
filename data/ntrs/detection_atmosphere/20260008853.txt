<p>EM41 Nonmetallic Materials and Space 
Environmental Effects Branch Overview
</p>
<p>The EM41 Nonmetallic Materials and Space Environmental Effects Branch, provides 
research, technology, and engineering support for space exploration related nonmetallic 
</p>
<p>materials, processes, and products along with a world class Space Environmental Effects 
(SEE) capability that provides exposure testing in simulated space environment conditions 
</p>
<p>and a levitation laboratory for investigators studying high- temperature materials. EM41 
supports, directs, and conducts nonmetallic material research and space environmental 
effects testing across the entire life-cycle of space exploration hardware, from lab-scale 
</p>
<p>development to flight hardware. </p>
<p></p>

<p>Subscale Solid Rocket Motor Processing/Manufacturing
</p>
<p>EM41 conducts subscale solid rocket motor 
material selection, testing, and processing. 
EM41 is leading the industry in developing 
alternative materials for solid rocket motor 
systems. EM41 maintains and operates a variety 
of capabilities that are used to verify the 
performance of new material and process 
options that help maintain environmental 
compliance and ensure mission success. 
</p>
<p>Tape-wrapping carbon phenolic nozzle billets
</p>
<p>24” SRTMV-BOLE-I1 Test Fire at East Test Area</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>The Plasma Torch Test Facility (PTTF) — an internationally 
unique ablation test facility used for material screening that 
offers reliable, statistically proven data at a fraction of the 
cost of subscale motors or other test beds. The PTTF 
simulates the erosive flow found in solid rocket motor 
plumes and exposes samples to a variable heat load and 
particle content impingement in order to measure the 
material's ablation performance.
</p>
<p>Plasma Torch Test Facility</p>
<p></p>
</div>
<div class="page"><p></p>
<p>Solid Fuel Torch (SFT) — a mid-range subscale test motor that can expose multiple materials to 
</p>
<p>solid rocket motor environments. SFTs offer customizable test sections, close to 60 second fire 
</p>
<p>times, and real time erosion data collection.
</p>
<p>Solid Fuel Torch</p>

<div class="page"><p></p>

</p>
<p>motor that provides qualification level thermal erosion 
</p>
<p>performance data typically required prior to full-scale solid

<p>Rubber Mill
</p>
<p>Using the Rubber Development Laboratory, EM41 conducts 
reformulation research and development on a variety of 
rubber materials. Six-pound mixes allow for rapid mixing, 
milling, and testing of candidate rubbers for a variety of 
applications at low expense in order to down-select 
formulations for larger scale mixing and testing. Rubber mixed 
in the Rubber Development Laboratory in small batches has 
chemical, mechanical, and thermal properties comparable to 
those of established formulations mixed on a large scale, 
making it an excellent sub-scale test-bed for formulation 
development. Any desired constituent of a rubber formulation 
can be varied in amount, or be substituted with a different 
material, giving a wide variety of formulation possibilities.
Analysis of cured rubber via SEM/EDS, Raman spectroscopy, 
and other chemistry methods (via internal MSFC and external 
providers).

<p>Automated Cleaning Facility (Waterblast)
</p>
<p>The Automated Cleaning Facility (ACF), sometimes referred to as 
Waterblast or Hydroblast Facility, is home to a High pressure water 
stripping system that allows stripping of hard to remove adhesives, 
paint systems, and even cladding and chromate conversion 
coatings. This process supports projects like the Space Launch 
System (SLS) by providing test coupon cleaning, de-painting, motor 
case cleaning, and hydro-proof pressure testing.
</p>
<p>This Facility also houses a fully integrated robot and turntable; two 
high pressure Waterjet pumps which operate at pressures of 
20,000/36,000 PSIG; and a Nitrojet 5000 System which has 
operational pressures of 1000 – 55,000 PSIG and is utilized in 
water-free stripping, precision cutting and cleaning, and cryogenic 
pressure testing.</p>
<p></p>

<p>Space Environment Effects (SEE)
</p>
<p>MSFC has world class SEE expertise and test facilities to recreate the 
</p>
<p>space environment in the lab.
</p>
<p>• Capabilities include individual and combined 
effects utilizing multiple unique test systems 
for the most complete SEE test capability 
available in the world.
</p>
<p>• MSFC’s ability to test all effects in one 
location is critical for minimizing the handling 
of sensitive material coupons after 
environmental aging.
</p>
<p>• Test systems can be rapidly adapted and 
reconfigured to customize tests to meet 
customers needs.
</p>
<p>• The SEE Team provides space-flight 
technology development programs the ability 
to elevate their hardware to TRL-6 
“Qualification in a Relevant Environment”.
</p>

<p>Space Environment Effects (SEE)
</p>
<p>SEE Team’s test systems can be used to support projects and materials for any space environment 
</p>
<p>from near earth to deep space.
</p>
<p>• Space environment is not just vacuum but includes a host of challenging conditions that affect 
</p>
<p>spacecraft materials and systems.
</p>
<p>• The space environment varies by location, for example, one material suitable for Low Earth Orbit 
</p>
<p>(LEO) might not survive in lunar and vice versa.
</p>
<p>• Testing will differ whether the spacecraft is in LEO, geosynchronous, lunar, Mercury, Mars, Jupiter, 
</p>
<p>etc. and how much solar activity is predicted for the mission.</p>

<p>Ionizing Radiation: High-Energy Electrons and Protons
</p>
<p>Combined Environmental Effects Facility (CEEF)--MSFC has two particle accelerators that produce electrons up 
</p>
<p>to 2.5 million electron volts (MeV) and protons up to 700 thousand electron volts (keV). The CEEF is used for testing 
</p>
<p>Radiation Effects on materials and components with an added cryogenic capability for Deep Space Missions.
</p>
<p>Side View of CEEF Top View of Target Chamber
</p>
<p>18-inch Beam Diameter
</p>
<p>• Charged particle radiation occurs naturally in space.
• While we typically think of radiation being harmful to humans 
</p>
<p>or causing single event effects in electronics, radiation can 
also damage materials. 
</p>
<p>• Exposure to radiation can embrittle polymers through cross-
linking or chain-scission.  It also degrades solar array 
performance.

<p>Plasma Environments Are Present Throughout 
The Universe 
</p>
<p>• Plasma is a rarefied gas composed of 
charged particles 
</p>
<p>• Ions or protons (+)
</p>
<p>• Electrons (-)
</p>
<p>• Plasmas interact with spacecraft surfaces 
very differently than neutral gases
</p>
<p>• Create spacecraft charging scenarios
</p>
<p>• The Sun is composed of plasma and is a 
source of plasma in our solar system
</p>
<p>• Solar Wind Plasma
</p>
<p>• Sun streams charged particles (radially) 
in all directions
</p>
<p>• Protons (+) and electrons (-)
</p>
<p>• Streaming Speed of 300 km/s (670,000 
mph)
</p>
<p>A high density plasma in the Marshall Magnetic Mirror</p>

in combination with other SEE exposures to evaluate combined 
effects.</p>
