<p>A New Test Methodology for Evaluating Fire Risk Due to Electrical Faults in Oxygen-
Enriched Environments 
</p>
<p>Authors: Stephen F. Peralta, Timothy D. Gallus, Gregory J. Harrigan, Thomas K. Evans, and 
DeAngelo D. Maestas 
</p>
<p>This paper discusses a new test methodology for assessing the safe use of materials found in 
spacecraft that may be exposed to electrical faults, as performed at the NASA White Sands Test 
Facility. “Wire-break” testing, modeled after the Cleveland Open Cup method, identifies the 
conditions under which pyrolyzed material transitions from a brief flash to a propagating flame. 
The approach focuses on fuel mass liberated through pyrolysis, mixed with oxygen, and ignited 
by a piloted ignition (spark due to fusion of wire) to achieve flame propagation with the least 
energy. The test approach attempts to find energy density thresholds derived using I²t values 
from current-induced heating of various test materials and wire characteristics. The application 
of the data provides a useful approach to mitigating fire risks in space systems by correlating 
threshold data to real-world designs and scaling factors, including oxygen pressure, wire 
diameter, heat sinking, and contained smoke volume. Several case studies, including Apollo 
missions and hypothetical scenarios, illustrate practical applications of these thresholds to 
enhance fire safety design considerations in space environments. </p>
