Two-Stage Autoignition of Isolated n-Dodecane Droplets in Varying Ambient 
Oxygen Concentrations in Microgravity

November 3rd-6th, 2021



Understanding the mechanisms involved during 
autoignition of liquid fuels is of importance in many 
practical combustion devices, as well as in fire safety.

Normal alkane fuel droplets undergo two-stage 
ignition under certain conditions (Tanabe et. al), with 
the first stage controlled by low-temperature 
chemistry, while the second controlled by 
intermediate and high temperature reactions.

Our earlier study investigated droplet autoignition in 
air at varying ambient pressures and temperatures 
under normal gravity (Nayagam et. al 2020)

This study explores the variations in autoignition 
delay times for varying ambient oxygen levels for n-
dodecane droplets in microgravity.

Experimental conditions ( 3atm, 650 K) chosen to get 
good separation between 1st and 2nd induction times

conditions

calculations

o Agrees with the idea that cool flame ignition starts in 
the fuel lean conditions

Concluding Remarks

o N-dodecane droplet autoignition characteristics were experimentally investigated for 
the first time in a microgravity environment. 

o First and second induction times, as well as initial cool flame propagation speed were 
obtained as a function of ambient oxygen concentration in an O2/N2 environment at 
fixed pressure (3 atm) and temperature (650 k).

o First induction time is insensitive to oxygen variations, while the second induction 
time varies as XO2

-2.6, for the range studied. 

o Homogeneous mixture calculations can be used to correlate the first induction time, 
but the second induction time depends on the effective equivalence ratio that seems 
to vary with the ambient oxygen concentration.

o A simplified theory seems to correlate the cool flame propagation speed, when the 
cool flame temperature is specified (from homogeneous calculations)
