<p>Fire and flame research in the Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR) on the International Space Station (ISS)
</p>
<p>Liquid droplets (2009-2017)
• 5 investigations conducted with the Multi-user Droplet
</p>
<p>Combustion Apparatus (MDCA).
• Tests in a still atmosphere, usually with a single floating
</p>
<p>or fiber-supported droplet.  Via fiber translation, some
tests with a linear array of droplets or with oxidizer ‘flow.’
</p>
<p>• Discovery of non-premixed cool flames!
</p>
<p>Gaseous fuels (2017-2022)
• 6 studies, with 4 different types of burners, conducted
</p>
<p>with the Advanced Combustion via Microgravity
Experiments (ACME) hardware.
</p>
<p>• Tests in a still atmosphere, sometimes of nitrogen-diluted
gaseous fuel with an oxygen-nitrogen mixture flowing
from the burner for an inverse flame.
</p>
<p>Solid fuels (2023-)
• 6 planned experiments using the Solid Fuel Ignition and
</p>
<p>Extinction (SoFIE) hardware.
• Tests typically conducted with an oxidizer flow that can
</p>
<p>enable upstream or downstream flame spread.
• Acrylic rods, sheets, and spheres are typical samples.
• Pulsed lighting reveals surface regression, bubbles, etc.
</p>
<p>• What? 17 experiments with laminar, non-premixed flames of gaseous, liquid, and solid fuels.
• Why? To improve spacecraft fire safety and practical combustion on Earth.
• When? CIR launched in 2008, and testing has been underway since 2009.
• Who? U.S. investigators, sometimes in collaboration with Italian, Japanese, and Russian researchers.
• Without CIR?  In Skylab, space shuttles, Mir space station, other ISS facilities, and Cygnus cargo spacecraft.
• CIR precursor? Combustion Module (CM) flew on STS-83 and STS-94 in 1997.  CM-2 flew on STS-107 in 2003.
</p>
